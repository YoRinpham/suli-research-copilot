\# 1. Product Summary



溯理 SULI is an AI-assisted scientific research workspace that helps researchers transform scientific literature into:



1\. structured evidence

2\. an interpretable research landscape

3\. unresolved or underexplored research gaps

4\. falsifiable hypotheses

5\. initial experiment plans



The product is designed around one central principle:



> Every important scientific claim generated by the system should be traceable to identifiable source evidence.



溯理 SULI is not intended to replace researchers.



Its purpose is to augment researchers' ability to:



\- process more scientific information

\- preserve research context

\- compare evidence across papers

\- identify disagreement and uncertainty

\- discover potentially valuable research directions

\- formulate testable hypotheses



The product should increase the scale and quality of scientific reasoning a researcher can manage without removing human judgment from the research process.



\---



\# 2. Product Vision



Scientific research increasingly suffers from an information-scale mismatch.



The volume of available literature grows faster than any individual researcher can reliably read, remember, compare, and synthesize.



Existing AI research tools often improve individual tasks such as:



\- paper summarization

\- translation

\- question answering

\- writing assistance

\- code generation



These improvements are useful, but they mainly optimize isolated steps.



溯理 SULI aims to address a deeper problem:



> Researchers need help maintaining and reasoning over the relationships between questions, claims, evidence, contradictions, gaps, hypotheses, and experiments.



The long-term vision is therefore not merely:



> AI that reads papers faster.



It is:



> An external scientific reasoning system that helps researchers convert distributed knowledge into evidence-backed research decisions.



For the hackathon MVP, this vision is deliberately constrained to one reliable end-to-end workflow.



\---



\# 3. Hackathon Positioning



This project is intended for the special research-efficiency track centered on Qwen-based scientific productivity, cognitive augmentation, and R\&D workflow innovation.



The project should demonstrate more than generic LLM productivity.



It should demonstrate at least one form of genuine cognitive augmentation:



\- increasing the amount of scientific evidence a researcher can reason over

\- exposing relationships that are difficult to maintain manually

\- identifying contradictions or unresolved questions

\- converting research gaps into testable hypotheses

\- maintaining explicit provenance between AI reasoning and scientific evidence



The project should avoid becoming only:



\- a PDF chatbot

\- a paper summarizer

\- an AI writing assistant

\- a generic RAG demo

\- a paper recommendation system



The primary innovation should come from the transformation:



> Literature → Evidence → Research Gap → Hypothesis → Experiment

\# 4. Target Users



\## 4.1 Primary Users



The MVP primarily targets:



\- graduate students

\- PhD students

\- university researchers

\- research engineers

\- early-career scientific researchers



\## 4.2 Initial User Profile



The initial user is assumed to:



\- already have a research question or research topic

\- have access to several relevant academic papers

\- understand the scientific domain better than the AI system

\- want help synthesizing literature and finding research directions

\- require traceability before trusting AI-generated scientific claims



\## 4.3 Non-Target Users for MVP



The MVP is not optimized for:



\- casual consumers reading popular science

\- fully autonomous laboratory operation

\- large enterprise R\&D portfolio management

\- journal editorial workflows

\- automatic manuscript publication

\- general-purpose academic search across the entire internet

\# 5. Core User Problem



A researcher working on a new topic often performs a workflow similar to:



Research question



→ search for relevant papers



→ download PDFs



→ read papers



→ highlight important passages



→ take notes



→ compare methods



→ compare datasets



→ compare results



→ remember disagreements



→ identify missing evidence



→ formulate research gaps



→ propose hypotheses



→ design validation experiments



The difficult part is not merely reading.



The difficult part is maintaining a coherent mental model across many pieces of distributed evidence.



This produces several recurring problems.



\---



\# 6. Key Problems



\## 6.1 Information Overload



A researcher may need to reason across:



\- dozens of papers

\- multiple competing methods

\- different datasets

\- inconsistent evaluation protocols

\- conflicting conclusions

\- experimental limitations

\- prior failed attempts



Human working memory is poorly suited to maintaining all of these relationships simultaneously.



\---



\## 6.2 Evidence Fragmentation



Relevant evidence is scattered across papers, sections, tables, figures, and experimental results.



Researchers frequently remember:



> "I saw a paper that said this."



but cannot immediately recover:



\- which paper

\- which section

\- which page

\- what experimental conditions applied



The product should reduce this context-recovery cost.



\---



\## 6.3 Summary Is Not Synthesis



A set of paper summaries does not automatically produce scientific understanding.



Researchers need to know:



\- which claims are repeatedly supported

\- which claims depend on specific assumptions

\- which results conflict

\- which variables have not been isolated

\- which comparisons are missing

\- where evidence is weak or incomplete



The product therefore must reason across papers, not merely summarize them independently.



\---



\## 6.4 Research Gap Discovery Is High-Cost



Identifying a meaningful research gap requires understanding:



\- what is known

\- what is disputed

\- what has been tested

\- what has not been tested

\- why existing evidence may be insufficient



This task is cognitively expensive and difficult to automate safely.



溯理 SULI should assist, not make authoritative declarations.



Research gaps generated by the system should therefore be presented as:



> evidence-backed candidate research gaps



rather than definitive statements of novelty.



\---



\## 6.5 AI Hallucination Is Especially Dangerous in Research



A scientifically plausible sentence is not equivalent to scientific evidence.



The system must avoid presenting unsupported generations as established fact.



If evidence is insufficient, the system should explicitly represent uncertainty.



Scientific trust requires provenance.



\---



\# 7. Product Goal



The product should help a researcher move from:



> "I have a research question and a collection of papers."



to:



> "I understand the major evidence, can inspect important disagreements, see candidate research gaps, and have several testable hypotheses with initial experiment plans."



The system should reduce cognitive overhead while preserving researcher control.



\---



\# 8. Core Product Loop



The MVP is organized around the following reasoning pipeline:



Research Question



→ Papers



→ Parsed Paper Content



→ Retrieved Evidence



→ Scientific Claims



→ Research Landscape



→ Conflicting / Missing Evidence



→ Candidate Research Gaps



→ Falsifiable Hypotheses



→ Experiment Plans



This pipeline is the backbone of the product.



Features that do not directly strengthen this workflow should generally remain out of scope for the MVP.



\---



\# 9. Core User Flow



\## 9.1 Create Research Project



The user creates a research project.



A project contains:



\- project title

\- research question

\- uploaded papers

\- extracted paper content

\- evidence

\- claims

\- candidate research gaps

\- hypotheses

\- experiment plans



\---



\## 9.2 Enter Research Question



The user defines the primary research question.



Example:



> Can retrieval augmentation reduce hallucination in multimodal large language models?



The research question becomes the main context used for:



\- retrieval

\- evidence selection

\- landscape generation

\- research-gap discovery

\- hypothesis generation



The system should allow the research question to be edited later.



\---



\## 9.3 Upload Academic Papers



The user uploads academic PDF files.



The system should:



1\. store the original file

2\. extract text

3\. preserve page provenance

4\. attempt to detect section headings

5\. split the paper into retrievable chunks

6\. create embeddings

7\. store structured metadata



The system should not silently discard parsing failures.



If parsing quality is low, the UI should expose that limitation.



\---



\## 9.4 Build Evidence Set



The system retrieves passages relevant to the user's research question.



A retrieved passage is not automatically evidence.



The system should distinguish between:



\- raw paper chunk

\- retrieved candidate passage

\- evidence selected for a scientific claim



Evidence should preserve at minimum:



\- evidence ID

\- paper ID

\- paper title

\- source page

\- section when available

\- source text

\- relation to the research question



\---



\## 9.5 Generate Research Landscape



Using retrieved evidence, the system generates a structured overview of the research landscape.



Possible outputs include:



\- major research approaches

\- recurring methods

\- commonly used datasets

\- repeated scientific claims

\- important evaluation metrics

\- major findings

\- areas of disagreement



Each important scientific claim should reference evidence IDs.



The user should be able to inspect the underlying source.



\---



\## 9.6 Surface Conflicting Evidence



The system should identify claims or findings that appear inconsistent across sources.



Example:



Claim:



> Retrieval augmentation reduces factual hallucination.



Supporting evidence:



\- E12

\- E18

\- E31



Conflicting or limiting evidence:



\- E44

\- E52



The system should not automatically declare which side is correct.



It should expose:



\- source conditions

\- methodological differences

\- possible reasons for disagreement

\- uncertainty



where those observations are supported.



\---



\## 9.7 Generate Candidate Research Gaps



The system proposes potential research gaps derived from the evidence landscape.



Supported gap categories may include:



\- contradictory findings

\- underexplored variable

\- missing comparison

\- limited dataset coverage

\- insufficient replication

\- methodological limitation

\- untested interaction

\- unexplored domain

\- unresolved causal explanation

\- evaluation inconsistency



Each candidate gap should contain:



\- concise description

\- gap category

\- why the gap may matter

\- evidence IDs supporting the gap

\- potentially conflicting evidence

\- uncertainty level

\- assumptions



The system should avoid claiming absolute novelty.



Preferred language:



> "The uploaded literature provides limited evidence regarding..."



Avoid:



> "No one has ever studied..."



unless the evidence base genuinely supports that conclusion.



\---



\## 9.8 Generate Falsifiable Hypotheses



The user selects a candidate research gap.



The system generates several candidate hypotheses.



Each hypothesis should contain:



\- hypothesis statement

\- rationale

\- supporting evidence IDs

\- conflicting evidence IDs when relevant

\- independent variable

\- dependent variable

\- control variables

\- falsification condition

\- key assumptions



The system should prefer hypotheses that are:



\- specific

\- testable

\- falsifiable

\- linked to available evidence



The system should avoid vague hypotheses such as:



> "Method A may improve performance."



A better form is:



> "Under condition X, increasing variable A from range Y to Z will reduce metric B relative to baseline C."



\---



\## 9.9 Generate Experiment Plan



The user selects one hypothesis.



The system generates an initial experiment design.



The experiment plan should contain:



\- objective

\- hypothesis under test

\- independent variable

\- dependent variable

\- control variables

\- experimental groups

\- baseline

\- dataset or sample requirements

\- evaluation metrics

\- expected observations

\- falsification criteria

\- potential confounding factors

\- risks or limitations



The experiment plan is a research planning aid.



It is not an authoritative scientific protocol.

\# 11. Evidence Provenance Model



Evidence provenance is a non-negotiable product requirement.



The system should preserve the chain:



Generated Scientific Statement



→ Claim



→ Evidence



→ PaperChunk



→ Paper



→ Page



The user should be able to move from an AI-generated claim back to the source passage with minimal effort.



The system must never fabricate:



\- paper references

\- page numbers

\- evidence IDs

\- citations

\- experimental results



If provenance cannot be established, the claim should be clearly marked as:



\- unsupported

\- inferred

\- speculative

\- insufficient evidence



as appropriate.



\---



\# 12. Human-in-the-Loop Principle



溯理 SULI assists scientific reasoning but does not own scientific judgment.



The user remains responsible for deciding:



\- whether evidence is relevant

\- whether a research gap is meaningful

\- whether a hypothesis is scientifically sound

\- whether an experiment design is appropriate

\- whether a conclusion is justified



The product should therefore support:



\- inspecting evidence

\- rejecting AI suggestions

\- selecting among candidate gaps

\- selecting among hypotheses

\- editing generated content



The system should not automatically progress through irreversible research decisions.



\---



\# 13. AI Behavior Requirements



\## 13.1 Evidence-Grounded Generation



Scientific claims should use retrieved evidence whenever possible.



\## 13.2 Structured Outputs



Important AI outputs should use structured schemas.



Examples:



\- claims

\- research gaps

\- hypotheses

\- experiment plans



\## 13.3 Uncertainty Representation



The system should distinguish:



\- supported observation

\- plausible inference

\- unresolved conflict

\- speculation



\## 13.4 No Fake Certainty



The system should not convert limited uploaded literature into universal scientific conclusions.



\## 13.5 Researcher Control



The model proposes.



The researcher decides.

\# 17. Product Principles



\## 17.1 Evidence First



Scientific reasoning must remain connected to source evidence.



\## 17.2 Researcher in Control



AI suggestions must remain inspectable and overridable.



\## 17.3 Structured Before Conversational



The system's core value comes from structured research reasoning, not chat UX.



Chat may exist as a secondary interface.



The core domain model must not be organized around chat messages.



\## 17.4 Reliability Before Sophistication



A stable single-model pipeline is preferable to an unreliable multi-agent architecture.



\## 17.5 Traceability Before Fluency



A slightly less elegant answer with strong evidence provenance is preferable to a fluent unsupported answer.



\## 17.6 Narrow Before General



A reliable workflow for a limited set of papers is preferable to weak support for every scientific field.



\## 17.7 Assistance Before Autonomy



The system should augment researcher cognition before attempting autonomous research.

\# 23. Final Product Definition



溯理 SULI should be understood as:



> An evidence-first AI research reasoning workspace that helps researchers transform scientific literature into structured evidence, candidate research gaps, falsifiable hypotheses, experiment plans, and reviewed analyses of uploaded data while preserving source provenance and human scientific judgment.

\## M3 scientific authority boundary

AI generation proposes Claims and Landscapes. AI semantic verification is an advisory critique with explicit risk flags and Evidence references. For Claim verification, the server derives displayed source excerpts from persisted Evidence; the verifier does not reproduce source text. Server validation enforces schema, references, provenance, state, and conservative conflict comparability; it does not establish scientific truth. The researcher makes the final judgment of whether a Claim is supported or inferred within the supplied corpus, then separately selects it for synthesis. A generated or revised Claim stays ineligible while that grounding review is pending. A generated or revised Landscape stays ineligible for later scientific stages until the researcher explicitly accepts its working synthesis. Human acceptance does not establish field-wide scientific truth. Human revisions require a fresh explicit review. Historical M3 model outputs are pending review after migration, without fabricated retrospective approval.

M4 begins with one current human-accepted Landscape. A ResearchGap is a candidate inference bounded to the supplied literature and carries explicit source relationships; its human selection indicates a research direction, not global novelty. A Hypothesis is a future-testable proposition with a falsification criterion, a selected Gap relationship, and motivating or challenging Evidence. Its AI assessment cannot select it. An ExperimentPlan tests one selected Hypothesis and starts pending human review; only an accepted current version is eligible for a future result workflow. M4 ends at that accepted plan and neither executes experiments nor analyzes results.

## M5 实验数据与结果分析

M5 从同项目、当前问题修订、未被替代且人工已接受的 ExperimentPlan 开始。研究者上传 UTF-8 CSV；系统保存不可变原始文件与 SHA-256，并生成可追溯的 DatasetVersion 和确定性数据画像。Qwen 可以建议清洗步骤和统计方法，但 CleaningPlan、AnalysisSpec 都需要研究者明确审核后才能执行。没有清洗需求时，研究者可以直接选择原始版本作为分析输入。清洗只执行白名单操作，产生新版本，并公开删除行数；异常值不会自动删除。

统计方法由服务端注册的确定性代码运行，每次执行保存 AnalysisRun 和 StatisticalResult；不同分析、失败和非预期结果都保留。图表从真实 DatasetVersion 及可选 StatisticalResult 渲染为 FigureArtifact。**LLM does not calculate statistical results. Computed statistics are produced by deterministic code from immutable dataset versions. AI interpretation is advisory until human reviewed.** Qwen 可在有界、无原始数据行的上下文中解释完整相关结果集；生成解释需人工接受或拒绝，文本修订产生新的待审核版本。接受解释仅表示它适合作为本项目的工作性解释，不证明假设为真，也不建立普遍因果结论。

M5 的终点是已审核的分析与解释产物。最终研究报告、提交打包和演示加固属于 M6；本阶段不生成报告。

## M6 最终交付

图表可由研究者在M5明确确认后永久删除对象及对应PNG文件。原始数据、统计结果、VisualizationSpec执行记录和历史科学文本不随之删除。引用已删除图表的解释和报告保留历史快照，但不再具备当前下游/正式导出资格；必须用现有结果与剩余图表重新生成、审核当前输出。系统不自动选择删除目标、不重算统计，也不静默改写已接受解释或报告的来源。

M6 在冻结的科学管线之上组装报告，不再新增科学推理权限。报告需要当前问题修订、当前人工接受的 Landscape/ExperimentPlan/ResultInterpretation、人工选定的 Gap/Hypothesis、合法 DatasetVersion、已成功的分析及其 StatisticalResult、真实 FigureArtifact 和可回溯的 Claim/Evidence/Paper/PDF 物理页。若来源缺失或变更，报告生成必须显式失败。每个报告存储确定性来源快照和 SHA-256；Qwen 只写带服务器来源令牌的简短叙述块，不能撰写权威统计值、文献元数据或普遍新颖性宣称。数值、图表和引文由服务端确定性呈现。

模型报告为待人工审核草稿。人工可接受、拒绝或新建待审核修订；修订不继承接受状态。最终导出仅限当前且未被替代的人工已接受版本；草稿导出显著标明身份。Markdown 与 HTML 是必需格式，PDF 非必需。效率基准使用版本化合成演示场景和真实计时事件；人工基线缺失时不显示节省时间或百分比。预检以匿名化状态显示数据库、迁移、存储、模型和网络可用性，不泄露凭证。合成演示资产必须明确标注，不能当作真实科学发现。
